# Threat Actors

A threat actor is an individual, group, or organisation that carries out or may carry out a malicious activity against a system, network, organisation, or individual.

Threat actors can be compared based on:

Whether they are internal or external
Their resources and funding
Their level of sophistication and capability
Their motivation

## Nation-State

A nation-state is a threat actor sponsored or supported by a government.

They generally have:

Significant funding
Highly skilled personnel
Advanced tools
Intelligence resources
Long-term objectives
Common motivations
Espionage
Intelligence gathering
Political influence
Military advantage
Cyber warfare
Disruption

Example : A government-sponsored group compromises another country's government systems to steal military intelligence.

Remember: Nation-states tend to be highly funded, sophisticated and mission-driven.

## Unskilled Attacker / Script Kiddie

An unskilled attacker has limited technical knowledge and often relies on tools, scripts and exploits created by others.

They may use:

Publicly available hacking tools
Automated scanners
Pre-written exploits
Online tutorials
Motivations
Curiosity
Recognition
Entertainment
Disruption
Malicious intent
Example

Someone downloads an existing DDoS tool and uses it against a website without understanding how the tool works.

Remember: Low skill does not necessarily mean low impact.

## Hacktivist

A hacktivist is an attacker motivated by political, social, religious, or ideological beliefs.

Common activities
Website defacement
DDoS attacks
Data leaks
Account compromise
Information disclosure

Motivation : Political or philosophical beliefs

Example : A group launches a DDoS attack against a government website to protest government policy.

## Insider Threat

An insider threat comes from someone who has legitimate access to an organisation's systems or information.

This could include:

Current employees
Contractors
Business partners
Former employees with remaining access

Insider threats can be:

Intentional
The person deliberately abuses their access.

Example : An employee steals customer data before leaving the company.

Unintentional
The person accidentally causes a security incident.

Example : An employee accidentally sends confidential information to the wrong recipient.

Motivations
Revenge
Financial gain
Espionage
Disgruntlement

## Organised Crime

Organised crime groups are professionally organised attackers who usually have financial motivations.

They may have:

Specialised teams
Significant funding
Criminal marketplaces
Malware developers
Access brokers
Common activities
Ransomware
Credential theft
Fraud
Data theft
Extortion
Motivation

Financial gain

## Shadow IT

Shadow IT refers to systems, applications, devices or services being used without the knowledge or approval of the organisation's IT/security team.

Example

An employee uploads company data to a personal cloud-storage account because it is easier to work from home.

Why is it dangerous?

Security teams may not be able to:

Monitor the system
Apply security controls
Patch the system
Control access
Protect the data

Important: Shadow IT is not necessarily malicious. It can create vulnerabilities because it exists outside normal security controls.

## Attributes of Threat Actors
Internal vs External

Internal
The threat actor has some form of legitimate access to the organisation.

Example:

Employee
Contractor
Partner

External
The threat actor operates from outside the organisation.

Example:

Cybercriminal
Hacktivist
Nation-state

## Resources and Funding

The amount of funding and resources available to an attacker affects what they can accomplish.

Low resources

May rely on:

Free tools
Public exploits
Automated attacks
High resources

May have:

Custom malware
Advanced infrastructure
Skilled employees
Zero-day exploits
Significant computing resources

## Level of Sophistication / Capability

Sophistication refers to how technically capable and advanced a threat actor is.

A sophisticated threat actor may:

Develop custom malware
Avoid detection
Exploit zero-days
Maintain persistence
Conduct long-term operations

## Threat Actor Motivations

### Data Exfiltration

Data exfiltration is the unauthorised removal of information from an organisation.

Examples:

Customer information
Passwords
Financial information
Intellectual property
Government documents

### Espionage

Espionage involves secretly obtaining information.

Often associated with:

Nation-states
Competitors
Intelligence operations

Example:

Stealing military or corporate research.

## Service Disruption

The goal is to make a system or service unavailable.

Example:

A DDoS attack overwhelms a company's web server.

## Blackmail

The attacker threatens to reveal information unless the victim meets their demands.

Example:

An attacker steals private company data and demands payment to prevent its release.

## Financial Gain

The primary goal is making money.

Examples:

Ransomware
Fraud
Credential theft
Extortion
Selling stolen data

## Philosophical / Political Beliefs

The attacker is motivated by an ideology or political cause.

Most commonly associated with:

Hacktivists

## Ethical

An attacker may believe their actions are justified by a moral or ethical cause.

This can overlap with hacktivism.

## Revenge

The attacker wants to punish an organisation or individual.

Often associated with:

Disgruntled employees
Former employees
Insiders

## Disruption / Chaos

The goal is simply to cause damage, confusion or instability.

The attacker may not have a financial objective.

## War

Cyber attacks may be conducted as part of military conflict.

Goals may include:

Disrupting infrastructure
Intelligence gathering
Damaging military systems
Creating economic disruption
